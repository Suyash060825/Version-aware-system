\begin{abstract}
Enterprise policies, operational protocols, and regulatory compliance mandates undergo continuous revision over time. Standard retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) paradigms typically treat knowledge repositories as static document collections, creating susceptibility to temporal version inversion—the erroneous retrieval of semantically relevant but legally superseded policy clauses. Furthermore, global index re-embedding during minor document updates incurs computational overhead, and routing deterministic inquiries unconditionally through autoregressive language models introduces unnecessary latency. This paper presents \textbf{PolicyLedger}, a systems architecture for version-aware enterprise policy retrieval. PolicyLedger formalizes temporal validity as closed calendar-date intervals $[\tau_s(v), \tau_e(v)]$ enforced alongside role-based access constraints prior to vector similarity scoring. An incremental compiler utilizing cryptographic chunk hashing and dynamic vector segment overlays confines re-indexing work to modified document fragments, achieving empirical update speedups between $616\times$ (1 modified chunk) and $6{,}504\times$ (zero-delta update) compared to full index rebuilds on an evaluated 138-chunk corporate policy corpus. An adaptive query router directs incoming inquiries across structured relational fact lookups (Tier~0), precompiled canonical question matching (Tier~1), cross-encoder reranked hybrid dense--sparse retrieval (Tier~2), and structural version diffing (Tier~3). On a 301-query held-out benchmark across 33 policy versions, PolicyLedger achieves an overall version selection accuracy of $82.72\%$ ($249/301$, 95\% CI: $[78.41\%, 86.71\%]$) and a composite median latency of $29.65\,\text{ms}$ (P95: $136.49\,\text{ms}$), with $54.15\%$ of queries resolving through sub-$25\,\text{ms}$ fast paths. While strict exact text generation accuracy is constrained to $27.91\%$ ($84/301$, 95\% CI: $[22.92\%, 33.22\%]$) under local 4-bit quantized generation, citation precision reaches a point estimate of $0.7973$ (bootstrap mean: $0.7934$, 95\% CI: $[0.7477, 0.8381]$). Post-hoc isotonic confidence calibration evaluated on unseen test data reduces Brier score by $50.5\%$ and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by $63.4\%$. In a 30-query cache evaluation, no stale, wrong-version, or cross-scope responses were observed. These empirical findings characterize the operational trade-offs of version-constrained retrieval in enterprise governance settings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{E}{nterprise} governance, corporate administration, information security, and regulatory compliance depend fundamentally on institutional policy documents. In organizational environments, policy corpora are non-stationary: corporate travel allowances, parental leave entitlements, remote work guidelines, and security controls are updated periodically. Each policy revision possesses an authoritative validity window bounded by discrete calendar dates $[\tau_s, \tau_e]$.

Despite widespread adoption across commercial environments, standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks \cite{lewis2020rag, gao2024rag_survey} treat target knowledge bases as static collections of unstructured text chunks. When deployed over versioned policy repositories, standard RAG pipelines face four distinct operational challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Version Inversion}: Semantic vector representations capture topical affinity but are inherently agnostic to discrete calendar validity boundaries. An inquiry regarding historical provisions (e.g., ``What was the domestic travel meal per diem in June 2023?'') risks retrieving semantically closer but legally superseded clauses from contemporary revisions, or vice versa \cite{huwiler2025versionrag}.
    \item \textbf{Index Rebuilding Overhead}: In conventional RAG implementations, modifying a single document clause often triggers full-corpus re-embedding and inverted-index reconstruction ($O(N \cdot d)$), creating computational bottlenecks in continuous document lifecycle workflows.
    \item \textbf{Inference Latency on Deterministic Lookups}: Standard RAG architectures route user queries indiscriminately to autoregressive Large Language Models (LLMs). This introduces seconds of generative latency for deterministic, standardized policy inquiries (e.g., ``What is the standard employee probation duration?'') that can be resolved via indexed relational lookups.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Department Authorization Leakage}: Without pre-retrieval scope enforcement, unpartitioned retrieval pipelines risk indexing and surfacing confidential departmental clauses to unauthorized users.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, this paper presents \textbf{\PolicyLedger}, a systems architecture for version-aware enterprise policy intelligence. PolicyLedger formalizes temporal validity as closed calendar-interval constraints ($[\tau_s(v), \tau_e(v)]$) applied alongside role-based access predicates \textit{prior} to similarity ranking. The system incorporates an incremental knowledge compiler based on cryptographic chunk hashing and dynamic FAISS segment overlays, paired with an adaptive four-tier query router that resolves structured inquiries without unconditional LLM synthesis.

\subsection{Research Questions}
This investigation is structured around four primary research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Temporal Version Selection)}: Can pre-retrieval calendar-interval filtering prevent temporal version inversion and reliably select the legally binding policy version across diverse query categories?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Incremental Update Efficiency)}: Can cryptographic chunk hashing and dynamic segment overlays decouple re-indexing latency from total corpus size during policy updates?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Multi-Tier Execution Latency)}: Can adaptive execution routing reduce end-to-end latency and unnecessary LLM invocations for deterministic policy inquiries while preserving citation grounding?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Access Control and Cache Isolation)}: Can pre-retrieval scope gating and composite cache key invalidation prevent cross-department data exposure and stale policy retrieval under dynamic updates?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions and Scope}
Rather than proposing a novel standalone embedding geometry or fundamental vector indexing algorithm, the primary contribution of this work is an \textbf{integrated systems architecture} for version-constrained enterprise knowledge retrieval. Specifically, this paper provides:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Invariant Formulation}: A closed-interval temporal validity model integrated into relational database predicates and vector search filters, achieving $82.72\%$ ($249/301$, 95\% CI: $[78.41\%, 86.71\%]$) correct version selection on a curated benchmark.
    \item \textbf{Incremental Knowledge Compiler}: An update engine utilizing SHA-256 chunk hashing and dynamic FAISS HNSW segment overlays, achieving update speedups of $616\times$ (1 modified chunk) to $6{,}504\times$ (zero-delta update) over full index rebuilds on a 138-chunk corporate policy corpus.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Four-Tier Execution Architecture}: A query router that navigates indexed relational fact lookups (Tier~0: $20.19\,\text{ms}$ P50), precompiled canonical QA vector matching (Tier~1: $23.28\,\text{ms}$ P50), cross-encoder reranked hybrid retrieval (Tier~2: $120.50\,\text{ms}$ P50), and structural version diffing (Tier~3: $128.14\,\text{ms}$ P50, $4{,}055.59\,\text{ms}$ P95).
    \item \textbf{Security Scoping and Grounding Verification}: A multi-factor confidence and cross-encoder Natural Language Inference (NLI) pipeline evaluated on held-out validation-to-test splits, reducing Brier score by $50.5\%$ and ECE by $63.4\%$.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Evaluation and Failure Mode Analysis}: Detailed empirical results across 301 test queries with 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals, strict chunk-level retrieval metrics, generator comparative analyses, and a 201-event systematic failure breakdown.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Literature Positioning}

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)}
RAG architectures augment parametric language models with non-parametric retrieval indices to ground text generation in external documents \cite{lewis2020rag, gao2024rag_survey}. Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) demonstrated dual-encoder efficiency \cite{karpukhin2020dpr}, while BGE embeddings provide dense semantic representations across diverse domains \cite{xiao2023bge}. BM25 lexical search remains complementary for exact keyword and acronym matching \cite{robertson2009bm25}. Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) \cite{cormack2009rrf} and cross-encoder rerankers (e.g., TinyBERT \cite{jiao2020tinybert, pradeep2023flashrank}) refine candidate ordering. However, standard RAG frameworks treat document collections as static, non-versioned text corpora.

\subsection{Temporal Information Retrieval and Evolving Documents}
Temporal Information Retrieval examines temporal expressions and timestamp metadata in document indexing \cite{campos2014temporal_ir}. Time-aware language models often append temporal strings to text prompts \cite{dhingra2022temporal_lm}. In the RAG literature, \textbf{VersionRAG} \cite{huwiler2025versionrag} directly addresses evolving corpora by constructing document version lineage graphs and version-aware embeddings. 

PolicyLedger differs fundamentally from VersionRAG in its architectural positioning: rather than modifying the continuous embedding space or relying on neural graph navigation, PolicyLedger formalizes temporal validity as discrete, closed calendar-date intervals ($[\tau_s(v), \tau_e(v)]$) enforced deterministically at the database and index pre-filtering layers. As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:related_work}, PolicyLedger combines this deterministic temporal model with pre-retrieval authorization scoping, cryptographic incremental compilation, multi-tier execution routing, and neural NLI verification.

\subsection{Vector Index Maintenance and Incremental Indexing}
Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) graphs \cite{malkov2020hnsw} and similarity libraries such as FAISS \cite{johnson2017faiss} enable scalable nearest-neighbor search. In dynamic document settings, modifying monolithic HNSW graphs requires edge rebalancing and node deletion. PolicyLedger utilizes SHA-256 chunk hashing paired with dynamic segment overlays and tombstone bitmasks, restricting re-embedding work strictly to mutated clauses.

\subsection{Confidence Calibration and Verification}
Deep neural networks and LLMs frequently exhibit uncalibrated overconfidence \cite{guo2017calibration}. In grounded generation, Natural Language Inference (NLI) cross-encoders \cite{he2021deberta} and self-reflective verification \cite{asai2024selfrag} assess whether retrieved evidence entails generated claims. PolicyLedger incorporates a DeBERTa-v3 NLI verifier and applies post-hoc isotonic regression fitted on validation data to calibrate output confidence.

\section{Problem Formulation and Invariant Theory}

Let $\mathcal{P} = \{P_1, P_2, \dots, P_M\}$ represent an enterprise policy repository of $M$ policies. Each policy $P_i$ consists of an ordered sequence of discrete versions:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{V}(P_i) = \{v_{i,1}, v_{i,2}, \dots, v_{i,K_i}\}, \quad K_i \ge 1.
\end{equation}
Each policy version $v \in \mathcal{V}(P_i)$ is formally defined by the 5-tuple:
\begin{equation}
v = \langle \tau_s(v), \tau_e(v), \mathcal{C}(v), D(P_i), \kappa(P_i) \rangle,
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $[\tau_s(v), \tau_e(v)] \subset \D$ is the closed validity interval over discrete calendar dates $\D$ (evaluated at day granularity);
    \item $\mathcal{C}(v) = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_{J_v}\}$ is the set of constituent textual chunks;
    \item $D(P_i) \in \mathcal{D} \cup \{\text{General}\}$ is the organizational department scope;
    \item $\kappa(P_i) \in \{\text{Public}, \text{Internal}, \text{Confidential}, \text{Restricted}\}$ is the confidentiality classification.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Temporal Validity Invariant}
For a query evaluated at target calendar date $t_q \in \D$, a chunk $c \in \mathcal{C}(v)$ belonging to policy version $v$ is temporally valid if and only if:
\begin{equation}
V(c, t_q) = 
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } \tau_s(v) \le t_q \le \tau_e(v), \\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:temporal_validity}
\end{equation}
When a superseding policy version $v_{k+1}$ is published with effective start date $\tau_s(v_{k+1})$, the system mutates the preceding boundary:
\begin{equation}
\tau_e(v_k) \leftarrow \tau_s(v_{k+1}) - 1\text{ day},
\end{equation}
enforcing that active validity intervals within policy $P_i$ remain mutually disjoint:
\begin{equation}
[\tau_s(v_j), \tau_e(v_j)] \cap [\tau_s(v_k), \tau_e(v_k)] = \emptyset \quad \forall j \ne k.
\end{equation}
\textit{Assumptions and Limitations}: The current mathematical formulation assumes non-overlapping sequential policy versions operating at discrete day granularity. Concurrent regional amendments, employee-specific exceptions, or overlapping validity windows require graph-structured extensions.

\subsection{Scope-Based Authorization Invariant}
Let $u$ denote a querying identity with department $D(u)$ and clearance set $K(u) \subseteq \{\text{Public}, \text{Internal}, \text{Confidential}, \text{Restricted}\}$. The pre-retrieval access predicate is:
\begin{equation}
A(c, u) = \I\left[D(P_i) \in \{D(u), \text{General}\} \wedge \kappa(P_i) \in K(u)\right].
\label{eq:auth_predicate}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Composite Retrieval Eligibility}
A candidate chunk $c$ is eligible for inclusion in the retrieval candidate pool if and only if both temporal and authorization predicates evaluate to 1:
\begin{equation}
E(c, u, t_q) = V(c, t_q) \cdot A(c, u) = 1.
\label{eq:eligibility}
\end{equation}
PolicyLedger enforces $E(c, u, t_q) = 1$ before similarity scoring, filtering out unauthorized or expired chunks prior to top-$k$ candidate ranking.

\subsection{Adaptive Routing Formulation}
Let $\mathcal{R} = \{\text{Tier}_0, \text{Tier}_1, \text{Tier}_2, \text{Tier}_3\}$ represent the available execution paths, each characterized by execution latency $L(r)$ and operational compute cost $C(r)$. The routing policy $\pi: \mathcal{Q} \rightarrow \mathcal{R}$ selects execution routes via heuristic rule predicates and similarity thresholds designed to optimize:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\pi} \mathbb{E}_{q \sim \mathcal{Q}} \left[ L(\pi(q)) \right] \quad \text{subject to } \mathrm{Acc}(\pi(q)) \ge \theta_{\mathrm{acc}}.
\end{equation}

\section{System Architecture and Implementation}

\subsection{Incremental Knowledge Compiler}
\label{sec:incremental_compiler}

To eliminate full-corpus re-embedding overhead during document updates, PolicyLedger implements an incremental compiler based on cryptographic chunk hashing.

\subsubsection{Chunk Identity and Hashing}
For each textual chunk $c$, the compiler computes a canonical SHA-256 digest over the normalized string, the active embedding model identifier, and the embedding model version string:
\begin{equation}
H(c) = \mathrm{SHA256}\left(\mathrm{norm}(c.\text{text}) \,\|\, \text{model\_id} \,\|\, \text{model\_version}\right),
\label{eq:chunk_hash}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{norm}(\cdot)$ standardizes Unicode casing and collapses contiguous whitespace. Embedding model identifiers are included to invalidate hashes if the underlying embedding backbone is upgraded.

\subsubsection{Delta Partitioning Algorithm}
When an updated policy version $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{new}}$ is ingested alongside prior version $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{old}}$, Algorithm~\ref{alg:incremental_compilation} partitions chunks into unchanged ($\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{unch}}$), modified ($\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mod}}$), added ($\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{add}}$), and deleted ($\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{del}}$) sets.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Incremental Knowledge Compilation}
\label{alg:incremental_compilation}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Prior chunks $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{old}}$, new chunks $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{new}}$, previous version ID $v_{\mathrm{prev}}$
\ENSURE Delta partition $\Delta = (\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{add}}, \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mod}}, \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{unch}}, \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{del}})$
\STATE Initialize hash map $M_{\mathrm{hash}} \leftarrow \{H(c) \mapsto c \mid c \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{old}}\}$
\STATE Initialize ID map $M_{\mathrm{id}} \leftarrow \{c.\mathrm{id} \mapsto c \mid c \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{old}}\}$
\STATE Initialize $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{add}} \leftarrow \emptyset, \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mod}} \leftarrow \emptyset, \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{unch}} \leftarrow \emptyset, \mathcal{M} \leftarrow \emptyset$
\FOR{each chunk $c \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{new}}$}
    \IF{$H(c) \in M_{\mathrm{hash}}$}
        \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{unch}} \leftarrow \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{unch}} \cup \{c\}$
        \STATE $\mathcal{M} \leftarrow \mathcal{M} \cup \{M_{\mathrm{hash}}[H(c)].\mathrm{id}\}$
    \ELSIF{$c.\mathrm{id} \in M_{\mathrm{id}}$}
        \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mod}} \leftarrow \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mod}} \cup \{c\}$
        \STATE $\mathcal{M} \leftarrow \mathcal{M} \cup \{c.\mathrm{id}\}$
    \ELSE
        \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{add}} \leftarrow \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{add}} \cup \{c\}$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{del}} \leftarrow \{c.\mathrm{id} \mid c \in \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{old}}\} \setminus \mathcal{M}$ \COMMENT{Tombstoned IDs}
\STATE Forward only $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{add}} \cup \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mod}}$ to FastEmbed ONNX embedding pipeline
\STATE Update dynamic segment overlay and record bitmask tombstones for $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{del}}$
\RETURN $\Delta = (\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{add}}, \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mod}}, \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{unch}}, \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{del}})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Computational Complexity Formulation}
Let $N = |\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{old}}|$ and $M = |\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{new}}|$. Initializing hash lookup maps requires $O(N)$ operations, while iterating through new chunks requires $O(M)$ hash lookups. Forward neural embedding operations are restricted strictly to modified and added chunks:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{embed}} = O(|\Delta| \cdot d), \quad \text{where } |\Delta| = |\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{add}}| + |\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mod}}|.
\end{equation}
The total incremental compilation time complexity is:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{total}} = O(N + M + |\Delta| \cdot d + I_{\Delta}),
\end{equation}
where $I_{\Delta}$ represents the cost of inserting $|\Delta|$ vectors into the dynamic FAISS segment overlay and updating BM25 posting lists. When $|\Delta| \ll N$, embedding compute is decoupled from total corpus scale.

\subsection{Adaptive Four-Tier Query Router}

\subsubsection{Tier 0: Structured Fact Resolution}
Inquiries targeting discrete quantitative or entity attributes (e.g., notice periods, expense limits) target structured relational triples $\langle\text{Subject}, \text{Predicate}, \text{Value}\rangle \in \mathcal{F}$ indexed in PostgreSQL. The Fact Resolver executes an indexed SQL lookup:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Lookup}(P_i, v, \text{predicate}) \rightarrow \langle\text{val}, \text{unit}, c_{\mathrm{source}}\rangle,
\end{equation}
resolving queries with measured median latency of $20.19\,\text{ms}$ without LLM generation.

\subsubsection{Tier 1: Canonical Question--Answer Matching}
During compilation, canonical question clusters are synthesized and embedded into a persistent FAISS HNSW index ($M=32, efConstruction=64$). At query time, query vector $\mathbf{q} \in \R^{384}$ is matched against canonical vectors $\mathbf{k}_j$:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{sim}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{k}_j) = \frac{\mathbf{q}^{\top} \mathbf{k}_j}{\|\mathbf{q}\|_2 \|\mathbf{k}_j\|_2} \ge \theta_{\mathrm{QA}} \quad (\theta_{\mathrm{QA}} = 0.80).
\label{eq:cosine}
\end{equation}
Valid matches satisfying scope filtering return verified answers and chunk citations at a measured median latency of $23.28\,\text{ms}$.

\subsubsection{Tier 2: Hybrid Dense--Sparse Retrieval with Grounded Generation}
Unstructured or complex inquiries route to hybrid retrieval:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dense Retrieval}: Query embedding $\mathbf{q}$ is matched against authorized chunk vectors in ChromaDB using cosine distance.
    \item \textbf{Sparse Retrieval}: BM25 lexical matching scores token overlap.
    \item \textbf{Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)}: Rankings are combined via:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{RRF}(c) = \frac{1}{k + r_d(c)} + \frac{1}{k + r_s(c)} \quad (k = 60).
    \label{eq:rrf}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Encoder Reranking}: Top-50 candidates are filtered by $E(c, u, t_q) = 1$ and reranked using FlashRank ($ms\text{-}marco\text{-}TinyBERT\text{-}L\text{-}2\text{-}v2$) to yield top-8 evidence chunks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Tier 3: Structural Version-Diff Comparison}
Inquiries requesting differences between policy revisions (e.g., ``What changed between v1.0 and v2.0 of the Remote Work Policy?'') trigger structural diffing. The engine parses the section AST structures of both versions, extracting clause additions, modifications, and removals.

\subsection{Security Threat Model and Defenses}
\label{sec:threat_model}

Enterprise policy retrieval operates under distinct adversarial threats:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Department Privilege Escalation}: An employee querying confidential HR or executive policies. \textit{Defense}: Pre-retrieval relational filtering ($A(c, u) = 1$) excludes unauthorized candidate chunks prior to vector distance scoring.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Inversion & Stale Retrieval}: Querying expired policy entitlements. \textit{Defense}: Strict calendar date filtering ($V(c, t_q) = 1$) invalidates obsolete versions.
    \item \textbf{Semantic Cache Poisoning & Cross-Scope Replay}: Replaying cached answers across users with differing clearances. \textit{Defense}: Composite cache key hashing $K_{\mathrm{cache}} = \mathrm{SHA256}(D(u) \,\|\, K(u) \,\|\, t_q \,\|\, \mathrm{norm}(q))$ and dynamic chunk status verification upon cache hit.
    \item \textbf{Direct & Indirect Prompt Injection}: User prompts containing jailbreak commands or malicious policy text. \textit{Defense}: Structural input sanitization, system prompt isolation, and cross-encoder NLI verification.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Neural Grounding Verification and Confidence Calibration}

\subsubsection{Neural NLI Verification}
To verify generated responses against retrieved evidence, candidate answers $A$ paired with evidence chunks $E$ are evaluated via a cross-encoder NLI model ($nli\text{-}deberta\text{-}v3\text{-}base$ \cite{he2021deberta}):
\begin{equation}
P(\text{Entailment} \mid E, A) \ge \theta_{\mathrm{entail}} \quad (\theta_{\mathrm{entail}} = 0.35).
\end{equation}
If contradiction probability exceeds entailment, the system triggers safe abstention.

\subsubsection{Confidence Calibration Formulation}
Raw confidence combines retrieval score $S_{\mathrm{ret}}$ and lexical surface coverage $S_{\mathrm{cov}}$:
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{raw}} = 0.55 \cdot S_{\mathrm{ret}} + 0.45 \cdot S_{\mathrm{cov}}.
\label{eq:confidence}
\end{equation}
To transform heuristic scores into empirical probabilities, post-hoc isotonic regression $f_{\mathrm{iso}}$ is fitted strictly on the validation set (\texttt{benchmark\_val.json}) and evaluated on the test set:
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{cal}} = f_{\mathrm{iso}}(C_{\mathrm{raw}}).
\end{equation}
Calibration error is measured via Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across $B=10$ bins:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECE} = \sum_{b=1}^{B} \frac{|B_b|}{N} \left| \mathrm{acc}(B_b) - \mathrm{conf}(B_b) \right|,
\end{equation}
and Brier score: $\mathrm{BS} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (C_i - y_i)^2$.

\section{Experimental Methodology}

\subsection{Hardware and Software Environment}
All benchmarks were executed on an isolated Linux host (kernel 7.1.8 x86\_64, 12 logical vCPUs, 6 physical cores, 31.06~GB RAM, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Laptop GPU with 3.68~GB VRAM). The software stack consists of Python 3.14.6, PyTorch 2.13.0, ChromaDB 1.5.9, FAISS 1.15.0, FastEmbed 0.8.0, FlashRank 0.2.10, PostgreSQL 16, Redis 7 (AOF persistence enabled), and local Ollama inference ($qwen3:4b\text{-}q4\_K\_M$). Cloud baseline experiments utilized Google Gemini 2.0 Flash via official API endpoints.

Policy documents were constructed based on standard corporate governance templates (HR leave, travel allowances, IT acceptable use, data security, remote work protocols).

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics and Statistical Methods}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Version Selection Accuracy}: Fraction of queries where the retrieved policy version matches the gold version.
    \item \textbf{Retrieval Metrics}: Recall@$k$, MRR@10, and NDCG@10 evaluated at policy-level and strict chunk-level.

    \item \textbf{Statistical Intervals}: 95\% confidence intervals computed via non-parametric bootstrap resampling ($n=10{,}000$ iterations).

\section{Discussion}

The empirical evaluation yields four primary observations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Invariant Gating}: Enforcing temporal intervals $[\tau_s, \tau_e]$ and authorization predicates $A(c, u)$ prior to vector similarity scoring prevents expired and out-of-scope candidates from entering the active retrieval pool, achieving $82.72\%$ version selection accuracy across the 301-query suite.
    \item \textbf{Update Efficiency Decoupling}: The incremental knowledge compiler restricts re-embedding compute to $O(|\Delta| \cdot d)$, delivering speedups between $616\times$ and $841\times$ for non-zero policy modifications on the evaluated corpus.
    \item \textbf{Execution Latency Trade-offs}: Directing $54.15\%$ of traffic through Tier~0 and Tier~1 fast paths reduced composite median latency to $29.65\,\text{ms}$, while complex structural version diffing in Tier~3 exhibited a $4{,}055.59\,\text{ms}$ P95 tail latency.
    \item \textbf{Grounding and Calibration}: While strict exact text generation accuracy was bounded at $27.91\%$ under local 4-bit generation, citation precision reached $0.7973$ (bootstrap mean: $0.7934$, 95\% CI: $[0.7477, 0.8381]$), and post-hoc isotonic calibration reduced test-set ECE by $63.4\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Corpus Scale and Domain}: The evaluation was conducted on 20 enterprise policies (138 chunks, 33 versions). Validating retrieval and temporal filtering across larger, multi-domain legal and regulatory corpora remains an essential requirement.
    \item \textbf{Benchmark Category Skew}: 89.37\% of the benchmark queries comprise canonical QA or fact lookups, favoring deterministic fast paths. Unstructured semantic queries ($N=8$) and temporal queries ($N=4$) represent smaller targeted stress tests.
    \item \textbf{Incremental Baseline Scope}: The incremental compiler was evaluated against a full index rebuild baseline; comparison against modern dynamic vector database upserts represents an important direction for future benchmarking.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Granularity Assumptions}: The current temporal validity model assumes discrete day granularity and mutually disjoint validity intervals, and does not model overlapping regional amendments or employee-specific grace periods.
    \item \textbf{Version Selection on Unanchored Queries}: The $12.5\%$ version accuracy on semantic queries highlights that queries lacking explicit date tokens require dedicated query-side temporal intent parsing.
    \item \textbf{Security Sample Size}: Cache isolation ($N=30$), adversarial refusal ($N=18$), and NLI verification ($N=14$) represent pilot evaluations; larger stress tests are required for broader safety validation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented \textbf{\PolicyLedger}, an enterprise policy intelligence architecture that integrates temporal validity interval constraints, role-based authorization gating, incremental SHA-256 knowledge compilation, and an adaptive four-tier query router. On a 301-query benchmark over a 138-chunk corporate policy repository across 33 versions, PolicyLedger demonstrated: (i) an overall version selection accuracy of $82.72\%$ ($249/301$, 95\% CI: $[78.41\%, 86.71\%]$); (ii) incremental compilation speedups between $616\times$ (1 modified chunk) and $6{,}504\times$ (zero-delta update) over full index rebuilds; (iii) a composite median response latency of $29.65\,\text{ms}$ (P95: $136.49\,\text{ms}$), driven by $54.15\%$ fast-path resolution; (iv) citation precision of $0.7973$ (bootstrap mean: $0.7934$, 95\% CI: $[0.7477, 0.8381]$); (v) post-hoc confidence calibration reducing test-set Brier score by $50.5\%$ and ECE by $63.4\%$; and (vi) zero observed stale, wrong-version, or cross-scope cache responses in a 30-query isolation evaluation. Future work will investigate query-side temporal intent extraction for unanchored semantic inquiries, graph-structured validity models for overlapping regulatory amendments, and streaming incremental indexing across massive corporate corpora.
