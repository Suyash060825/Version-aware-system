Veritas: Version-Aware Enterprise Policy Intelligence
via Incremental Knowledge Compilation and
Adaptive Multi-Tier Retrieval
Suyash Pradhan

Abstract—Enterprise policy repositories undergo continuous
revision, requiring historical and active provisions to coexist under
strict role-based access. Conventional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) models rank evidence primarily by semantic similarity,
risking temporal version inversion, full re-indexing overhead,
generative latency on deterministic lookups, and authorization
leakage. We present Veritas, an enterprise policy intelligence
system that treats retrieval as a constrained systems problem:
a composite eligibility predicate E(c, u, tq ) = V (c, tq ) · A(c, u)
enforces date-bounded validity and role authorization prior
to evidence admission and reranking. Veritas integrates a
seven-stage SHA-256 incremental compiler, an adaptive fourtier query router, and a calibrated NLI verification layer. We
evaluate Veritas across two clearly delineated regimes: (1) a
frozen 301-query baseline benchmark (commit 61d64b71; 21
policies, 128 chunks), achieving 100.0% authorization correctness
(0/301 leaks), 88.07% answered-query version selection accuracy
(96/109), and 54.15% deterministic fast-path routing with 29.65 ms
median latency; and (2) an expanded 922-query characterization
suite (commit 449e69b3; 120 policies, 3,000 chunks, 32 user
archetypes), demonstrating zero observed unauthorized evidence
leaks across 3,840 evaluations (95% upper bound < 0.08%), 63.4%
deterministic/refusal paths, 24.95× compiler speedup on 5%
amendment deltas across 3,000 chunks (95.0% compute avoided),
strict chunk-level Recall@10 of 98.29%, and 1.94 ms end-to-end
median latency at enterprise scale. Isotonic calibration achieves
0.041 validation ECE (0.000 on fit set), rising to 0.2954 under
runtime distribution shift. These results demonstrate that preretrieval constraint enforcement provides verifiable data isolation
and latency reduction for enterprise policy QA.
Index Terms—Retrieval-augmented generation, temporal information retrieval, version-aware retrieval, incremental indexing,
enterprise knowledge management, adaptive query routing,
confidence calibration, natural language inference.

policy documents that undergo continuous revision. Corporate
travel allowances, parental leave provisions, remote-work
rules, and security controls each undergo periodic amendment,
requiring retrieval mechanisms that distinguish historical validity from current applicability while respecting authorization

Conventional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architectures rank semantic similarity over document collections [1],
[2]. Within enterprise policy repositories, four operational risks
are prominent: (1) Temporal version inversion: superseded

Scope leakage: unpartitioned retrieval exposes confidential

This paper investigates: Can an enterprise policy retrieval
architecture simultaneously enforce date-bounded temporal
validity and role-based authorization constraints while reducing
unnecessary generation latency and incremental compilation
overhead, without degrading retrieval accuracy on legitimate
requests?
To answer this question, Veritas treats policy retrieval as
a constrained systems problem. While individual components
such as dense embeddings, lexical indexes, cross-encoders, and
NLI classifiers exist in isolation, Veritas formulates temporal validity intervals, role authorization, cryptographic delta
compilation, and tiered routing as an integrated architecture
where constraints are evaluated before candidate evidence enters
reranking or generative prompt assembly.
The technical contributions of this work comprise:
1) Composite eligibility predicate: closed date-bounded
validity intervals and role-derived authorization enforced
at every pipeline tier before evidence admission and
reranking.
2) Seven-stage incremental compiler: SHA-256 chunk
hashing, delta partitioning, targeted embedding, and
dynamic vector overlays, reducing dominant embedding
complexity from O(N · d) to O(|∆| · d).
3) Adaptive four-tier router: fact resolution (Tier 0),
canonical QA matching (Tier 1), hybrid dense-sparse
retrieval (Tier 2), and structural version diffing (Tier 3),
resolving deterministic traffic without generative LLM
overhead.
4) Security and evidence controls: pre-reranking scope
gating, NLI entailment verification, post-hoc calibrated
confidence, safe abstention, and tamper-evident audit
traceability.
5) Dual-regime empirical characterization: systematic
evaluation across a 301-query baseline and an expanded
922-query characterization suite, demonstrating zero
observed leakage across 3,840 authorization decisions,
strict chunk and policy retrieval characterization, 24.95×
compilation speedup on 5% amendment deltas, 0.962

II. R ELATED W ORK AND L ITERATURE P OSITIONING
A. RAG and Retrieval Quality
RAG grounds language generation in retrieved evidence
[1]. Dense representations demonstrated high efficacy [3],
advanced by contrastive text embeddings such as BAAI/bgesmall-en-v1.5 [4]. Lexical retrieval is grounded in the BM25
probabilistic framework [5], while Reciprocal Rank Fusion
(RRF) combines ranked lists without score calibration [6].
Cross-encoders distill token-level interactions for reranking [7],
deployed via the lightweight FlashRank library [8]. Evaluation
frameworks emphasize separate measurement of retrieval
quality, faithfulness, and answer relevance [18]–[20]. Veritas
applies temporal and authorization filtering before reranking,
invoking autoregressive generation only when deterministic
fast paths are insufficient.

B. Temporal Retrieval and Evolving Documents
Temporal information retrieval models time expressions and
temporal relevance [9], extended by time-aware language models [10]. VersionRAG explores RAG over evolving documents
via version lineage graphs [11]. TimelyRAG [12] incorporates
temporal distance directly into retrieval ranking for overlappingevolving documents. Veritas differs by formulating temporal
validity as closed calendar interval invariants [τs , τe ] enforced
prior to candidate admission and reranking rather than probabilistic attention, while integrating role-based authorization
scoping, SHA-256 incremental compilation, and adaptive multitier routing.

III. P ROBLEM F ORMULATION
Let P = {P1 , . . . , PM } denote the enterprise policy repository. Each policy consists of an ordered set of versions
V(Pi ) = {vi,1 , . . . , vi,Ki }. Each version is represented as a
5-tuple v = ⟨τs (v), τe (v), C(v), D(Pi ), κ(Pi )⟩, where [τs , τe ]
is the validity interval, C the policy chunks, D(Pi ) the organizational scope, and κ(Pi ) the confidentiality classification. For
currently active policy versions, τe (v) = ∞ (implemented as
9999-12-31).
For query date tq ∈ D, the Temporal Validity function is:
V (c, tq ) = 1[τs (v) ≤ tq ≤ τe (v)].
(1)
When superseding version vk+1 is published, the boundary
mutation rule τe (vk ) ← τs (vk+1 ) − 1 day enforces disjoint,
non-overlapping validity intervals within any single policy.
For user u, the Scope Authorization function is:
A(c, u) = 1[D(Pi ) ∈ {D(u), General}] · 1[κ(Pi ) ∈ K(u)],
(2)
where K(u) is the set of clearance levels granted to u.
The Composite Eligibility Predicate is:
E(c, u, tq ) = V (c, tq ) · A(c, u).
(3)
In deterministic routes (Tiers 0 and 1), E(c, u, tq ) filters
candidate generation directly within relational and FAISS
indexes. In hybrid retrieval (Tier 2), E(c, u, tq ) = 1 is
enforced immediately on initial candidates prior to crossencoder reranking and prior to neural context assembly,
ensuring that superseded or unauthorized text never enters
the LLM prompt. The system optimization objective is:
min E[L(π(q))] subject to Accsafe (π) ≥ θacc ,

is the composite decision accuracy over served answers and
valid abstentions. Failed validity or authorization checks result
in deterministic safe abstention.

VI. E MPIRICAL R ESULTS AND A NALYSIS
A. Security and Pre-Retrieval Authorization Scope Enforcement
Table III reports security performance. In the baseline
evaluation [Baseline, N = 301], 109 queries target authorized
provisions while 192 represent out-of-scope departments,
restricted confidentiality tiers, or adversarial jailbreaks. An
unrestricted baseline evaluates retrieval without pre-reranking
eligibility gating, resulting in 160 authorization violations
(53.16% unsafe exposure rate). In contrast, Veritas achieves
100.0% authorization/scope decision correctness (301/301)
with zero unauthorized evidence leakage (TP = 109, TN =
192, FP = 0, FN = 0).
In the expanded characterization suite [Characterization, N = 3, 840 evaluations across 32 user archetypes
× 120 policies], no unauthorized candidate evidence
was observed entering the reranker or prompt context (1, 840 TP, 2, 000 TN, 0 FP, 0 FN; one-sided 95% upper
bound < 0.08%). On authorized answerable baseline queries,
Veritas achieved 88.07% version accuracy (96/109 in the
frozen baseline benchmark), confirming access isolation without
degrading legitimate retrieval.

VIII. L IMITATIONS AND T HREATS TO VALIDITY

R EPRODUCIBILITY S TATEMENT

1) Tier-0 routing fallthrough. The Tier-0 fact router
requires explicit entity-predicate syntax; natural language
structured fact queries lacking exact keywords frequently
fall through to Tier-2 HYBRID RAG, incurring neural
retrieval latency.
2) Strict chunk vs. policy recall. Strict passage-level
Recall@1 reaches 9.69% (N = 877), while Recall@10
reaches 98.29%, indicating that single-chunk precision
remains challenging in dense corpora.
3) Candidate pool depth sensitivity. Highly constrained
multi-clause queries experience candidate starvation at
K = 50 (84.0% recall), requiring expanded candidate
beams (K = 100) with associated latency tradeoffs.
4) Synthetic corpus scale. The 120-policy, 3,000-chunk
corpus models enterprise structures synthetically; evaluation on proprietary production corporate repositories
remains future work.
5) Natural language temporal ambiguity. Unanchored
temporal expressions (e.g., “prior to recent changes”)
occasionally cause version ambiguity, whereas explicit
dates achieve 100% version resolution.
6) Concurrency lock contention. Multi-user throughput
saturates near 137 QPS under 50–100 concurrent workers
due to thread-lock synchronization overhead.
7) Citation granularity gap. Citation recall (0.2487) reflects broad section-level chunk retrieval evaluated against
sentence-level gold annotations.
8) Calibration distribution shift. Calibration-split ECE
(0.041) versus runtime ECE (0.2954) demonstrates empirical sensitivity to open-domain query distributions.
9) NLI sample size. Pilot NLI validation used n = 14 pairs
and contradiction evaluation used n = 20 cases; broader
validation across thousands of clause pairs is ongoing.
10) Model quantization. Local LLM exact answer match
(27.91%) is partly constrained by 4-bit quantization of
the qwen3:4b backend.
